# Literature Review

## Overview
The design of this AR-based learning system is informed by three complementary strands of educational research: multimedia learning theory, spatial visualization research in STEM education, and embodied cognition in immersive learning environments. Together, these perspectives justify the use of 3D molecular visualization and AR timelines to support conceptual understanding, retention, and transfer.

## Mayer's Multimedia Learning Theory
Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning argues that learning is most effective when information is presented in a way that supports learners' cognitive processing without overloading limited working memory (Mayer, 2009). Central to the theory are several principles relevant to this project:

- Dual-channel processing: learners process visual and verbal information through separate channels, which means visuals and narration should be aligned rather than competing.
- Limited capacity: complex text-heavy presentations can overwhelm learners; spatial and animated representations can reduce extraneous cognitive load when designed carefully.
- Active learning: learners benefit when they are actively engaged in integrating words and images into a coherent mental model.

In this study, 3D molecular visualizations provide a meaningful visual representation of abstract scientific concepts, while AR timelines structure health-related information in a temporal sequence that complements textual explanations. The goal is not simply to add more media, but to present information in ways that reduce cognitive overload and improve schema construction.

## Spatial Visualization and STEM Outcomes
Research on spatial ability consistently shows that proficiency in spatial reasoning is associated with performance in STEM disciplines, especially in chemistry, engineering, and related fields (Sorby, 2009; Alias & Gray, 2007). Students with stronger spatial visualization skills are better able to rotate, compare, and mentally manipulate structures, which is essential when interpreting molecular geometry, structural relationships, and scientific diagrams.

The literature suggests that strengthening spatial visualization can improve conceptual understanding in science domains. In chemistry, for example, students must mentally transform 2D representations into 3D structures and connect symbolic notation to real-world spatial relationships. Traditional instructional formats often fail to support this transformation effectively, especially for learners who struggle with mental rotation.

This project directly addresses that gap by using 3D molecular visualization in an AR environment. Rather than asking learners to infer a molecule's structure from a flat image, the system provides an interactive, manipulable representation that supports the development of spatial reasoning and representation-based understanding.

## Embodied Cognition and AR
Embodied cognition suggests that learning is strengthened when learners interact with the world through physical and perceptual experiences, not just through abstract symbolic representations (Wilson, 2002). In this framework, cognition is shaped by bodily engagement, environmental context, and sensorimotor interaction.

AR is particularly well aligned with embodied cognition because it situates digital content in the learner's physical space and supports direct manipulation. When users move around a 3D molecule or observe a timeline anchored to a real environment, the learning experience becomes more spatially grounded and memorable. Physical interaction has been linked to better retention, deeper conceptual understanding, and improved transfer because the learner is not merely observing information but actively engaging with it.

For this project, the AR environment provides two embodied advantages:

1. Spatial embodiment: learners can rotate, inspect, and approach molecular structures in a three-dimensional space, supporting mental model formation.
2. Temporal embodiment: the AR timeline allows learners to navigate health events sequentially in a way that makes causal and chronological relationships more concrete.

This embodied interaction is especially relevant for the project’s dual focus on STEM visualization and health literacy, where students benefit from both spatial understanding and sequence-based comprehension.

## Why These Theories Support the Design
The system's design choices are grounded in the theoretical perspectives above:

- 3D molecular models are used because spatial visualization is a key determinant of STEM learning outcomes and can improve representational understanding of complex structures.
- AR is adopted because it supports embodied interaction and helps learners construct mental models through direct spatial manipulation rather than passive viewing.
- Timeline-based health information is incorporated because temporal organization supports memory retention and coherent understanding of cause-effect relationships.
- Visual and verbal information are intentionally paired to align with Mayer's multimedia principles, reducing extraneous cognitive load while preserving meaningful explanatory support.

Together, these theories support the argument that AR-supported learning environments can improve spatial reasoning, increase retention, and foster meaningful learning when instructional design is grounded in cognitive and embodied principles.

## Conclusion
The literature indicates that effective learning depends on how information is represented, how learners interact with it, and how well instructional design supports mental processing. Mayer's theory provides a cognitive framework for integrating visuals and text without overloading working memory; spatial visualization research demonstrates the importance of 3D reasoning in STEM; and embodied cognition explains why AR can be particularly powerful when learners manipulate content in physical space.

These theories justify the project’s design and provide a strong foundation for evaluating whether 3D molecular visualization and AR time-based learning enhance both understanding and retention relative to conventional 2D instructional approaches.
