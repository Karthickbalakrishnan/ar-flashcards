# Study Design Document

## Title
Evaluating the effects of 3D molecular visualization and AR timeline-based learning on spatial reasoning and health literacy retention.

## Objective
To assess whether augmenting learning materials with 3D molecular visualization and AR-supported health timelines improves both cognitive performance and knowledge retention compared with conventional instructional methods.

## Research Questions
1. Does 3D molecular visualization improve spatial reasoning performance relative to 2D static representations?
2. Does an AR-based timeline improve health literacy retention compared with a non-temporal or text-only presentation?
3. How do cognitive load and usability influence learning outcomes across conditions?

## Study Design
A randomized controlled trial with a pre-test/post-test/follow-up design, using a parallel-group comparison.

### Participants
- Target sample: 30-100 participants, depending on pilot or full-study scope
- Inclusion criteria: adult learners with basic digital literacy and no prior formal advanced chemistry or medical training required for the pilot
- Exclusion criteria: severe visual impairment affecting AR use without accommodation

### Conditions
- Condition A: 2D static molecular learning + text-based health information
- Condition B: 3D molecular visualization + text-based health information
- Condition C: 2D molecular learning + AR timeline for health information
- Condition D: 3D molecular visualization + AR timeline for health information

### Procedure
1. Recruitment and informed consent
2. Baseline demographics and prior knowledge survey
3. Pre-test assessment of spatial reasoning and health literacy
4. Random assignment to condition
5. Intervention session using assigned materials
6. Post-test assessment immediately after intervention
7. Delayed retention assessment 1-2 weeks later
8. Surveys for NASA-TLX and SUS

## Ethical Considerations
This study should be reviewed under institutional research standards. If applicable, investigators should obtain IRB approval before participant recruitment. The protocol should include:
- informed consent language
- data anonymization procedures
- optional withdrawal at any time without penalty
- secure storage of de-identified results

## Data Collection
- Knowledge tests: pre-test, post-test, delayed retention
- Spatial reasoning tasks: object rotation, mental model reconstruction, molecule-to-structure matching
- Health literacy tasks: recall, sequencing, concept application, and risk interpretation
- Subjective measures: NASA-TLX (cognitive load), SUS (usability), qualitative feedback

## Analysis Plan
- Use repeated-measures ANOVA or mixed-effects models to estimate condition effects over time
- Compare group differences in learning gain and retention
- Assess moderation by prior knowledge and self-reported engagement
- Report effect sizes and confidence intervals

## Limitations
- Small samples may limit generalizability
- AR performance may vary by device quality or participant familiarity with technology
- Learning effects may be influenced by content complexity and baseline ability

## Deliverables
- Participant recruitment materials
- Consent form template
- Assessment instruments
- Codebook and analysis script
- Summary report with preliminary findings
