# Methodology

## Study Context

This project explores the effect of immersive educational interfaces on conceptual learning and engagement in science education. The prototype integrates AR visualizations and flashcard-based learning to support learning in anatomy and chemistry.

## Research Design

A mixed-methods evaluation can be used to assess the prototype, with an emphasis on learning outcomes and user experience.

### Suggested Study Design
- between-subjects or within-subjects comparison
- compare standard 2D instructional condition vs. AR/3D immersive condition
- incorporate short-term and delayed recall assessment

## Participants

Suggested sample:
- 20–50 participants
- undergraduate or graduate students
- mixed STEM and non-STEM backgrounds
- basic familiarity with mobile interfaces required

## Conditions

### Condition A: Traditional learning
- static 2D explanation
- textbook-like representation
- standard flashcard interface

### Condition B: Immersive learning
- AR + 3D model interaction
- zoom, rotate, and inspect scientific structure
- interactive timeline and concept-based review

## Materials

- anatomy concept cards
- chemistry molecular structure cards
- organ models and educational explanations
- AR target markers
- mobile web app interface

## Procedure

1. Participants complete a pre-test measuring baseline knowledge.
2. Participants are assigned to one condition.
3. They complete a short learning session using the assigned interface.
4. Participants complete a post-test on conceptual understanding.
5. Participants complete usability and workload ratings.
6. Participants participate in a brief interview about perceived learning, engagement, and difficulties.

## Measures

### Learning Outcomes
- pre/post knowledge test
- concept comprehension score
- delayed recall score
- transfer question performance

### Usability
- System Usability Scale (SUS)
- perceived ease of use
- perceived clarity of representations

### Cognitive Load
- NASA-TLX
- mental effort experienced during learning

### Engagement / Presence
- perceived immersion
- perceived usefulness
- subjective enjoyment and interest

### Interaction Metrics
- number of model rotations
- time spent on tasks
- reveal-to-answer latency
- interaction frequency across learning cards

## Data Analysis

- compare mean test scores across conditions
- calculate learning gain
- analyze correlation between interaction behavior and learning outcomes
- report usability and cognitive load results
- perform qualitative thematic analysis of interview responses

## Expected Findings

The study may reveal that:
- AR and 3D interactive systems help learners reason about spatial structures better
- embodied interaction improves engagement and memory retention
- multimodal educational design supports conceptual understanding more than static presentation alone

## Ethical Considerations

- informed consent
- voluntary participation
- data anonymization
- no harmful or misleading educational claims
- transparent explanation of study purpose

## Limitations

- small sample size may limit generalizability
- learning effects may vary by domain familiarity
- interface quality may influence engagement independently of learning gains

## Significance

This methodology allows the prototype to be framed as a serious HCI research artifact rather than a technical demo alone. It positions the project around measurable learning outcomes and interaction quality, which is essential for graduate-level research credibility.
